Kentucky Food Insecurity

County-level food insecurity rates for all 120 Kentucky counties, 2020–2023.

About This Project

Kentucky Food Insecurity tracks how many residents in each Kentucky county can't consistently afford enough food for an active, healthy life. It covers all 120 counties from 2020 to 2023, with the most recent figures reflecting 2023 conditions.

Each county's rate is shown against the statewide average, so it's easy to see which counties sit above or below it. A plain-language explainer covers what food insecurity means and how the rates are calculated.

How It Works

Pick a year, sort counties alphabetically or by rate, and search for a specific county. A toggle switches between the overall food insecurity rate and the child food insecurity rate (children under 18).

Built with vanilla HTML, CSS, and JavaScript.

Data source: Feeding America, Map the Meal Gap (2022–2025 editions, covering 2020–2023). Full academic citations are listed on the live site.

FAQ

Q: What is food insecurity?
A: A person is food insecure when they can't consistently afford enough food — often because they have to choose between groceries and other basics like rent, utilities, or medical bills.

Q: Are these direct counts?
A: No. They're modeled estimates from Feeding America, based on county-level data on poverty, unemployment, homeownership, and disability.
